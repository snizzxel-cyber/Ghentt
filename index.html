Ground floor · ±0.00 m

The nave stays public

No walls in the nave at street level. A 208 m² reading hall on the original tiled floor, open to the whole neighbourhood, with a café in the old choir.

Level +1 · 2.90 m

Eighteen rooms to focus

Fourteen study rooms sit on the old aisle galleries and look through the arcade into the reading hall. Four more are on the ground floor.

Levels +2 and +3 · 5.60 and 9.00 m

Home is above the books

Twenty student rooms rest on the arcade arches. On level +2 every room reaches out under the old aisle roof into an alcove with its own roof window, about 19 m² in all.

Level +4 · 12.30 m

Dinner under the ridge

No bedrooms in the attic. Instead one 132 m² kitchen for all residents, a terrace cut into the red tile roof, and 67 m² more on top of the café building.

Every level

Zero ramps, one lift

Every new floor meets the church at exactly the same height: 2.90, 5.60, 9.00 and 12.30 m. One lift in the glazed wing stops on all five levels.

Concept · section through the nave

Five levels, from public to private

The higher you go, the more private the building becomes. The ground floor belongs to everyone, the study rooms to the city’s students, the bedrooms to the residents, and the kitchen under the ridge is the shared living room.

    The church stays one space

    Nothing is built into the nave at ground level. The tiled floor stays visible, the organ gallery is kept, and the last bay stays open up to the roof, so the organ and the east wall remain visible from the reading hall.

    Everything new stands at the west end

    Café, bathrooms, lift, stair and bicycle store sit in two new buildings and a glazed wing next to the choir. From Voskenslaan the church looks almost as it does today; only dormers and the small roof terrace are new.

    Levels without ramps

    The floors of the new building sit at 2.90, 5.60, 9.00 and 12.30 m, exactly level with the church floors, so the three bridges through the west gable are flat. The lift in the glazed wing stops on all five levels; only the study rooms on level +1 are reached by stair.

        Living together

        A house manager on site. One contact for moving in, keys, repairs and study-room bookings. The library below has its own operator.

        Quiet, because people read downstairs. The reading hall and the living floors sit on top of each other, so good impact-sound insulation and clear quiet hours (for example 10 pm to 7 am) are part of the concept.

        Separate access. Residents use a transponder for the glazed wing and their own level. Library visitors stay on the ground floor; the café storage on level +1 is staff only.

        The kitchen as the meeting place. With one kitchen, all 20 residents meet there. A kitchen rota and fixed food lockers keep order, and a shared dinner once a week holds the community together.

        What we do not know yet. Who runs the rooms (a university, a foundation or a private operator) is still open. The rules above are a proposal.

        The contract

        Student lease

        Under the Flemish residential lease decree (studentenhuurovereenkomst). For enrolled students.

        Term

        Proposed: 12 months from September. For exchange students also one semester, if rooms are free.

        Deposit

        At most two months’ rent, without the service charge, on a blocked account in the tenant’s name. Up to €930 at the standard rate.

        Requirement

        Enrolment at a higher-education institution in Ghent, shown when the lease is signed.

        Legal framework as described by vlaanderen.be: Studentenhuurovereenkomsten. Check the current rules before signing.

        How to get a room

        1. Register your interest

          A short form with your course, the move-in date you want and your rate. For the social rate, add proof of income.

        2. See the house

          First virtually with the 3D tour on this page, then at a viewing on site.

        3. Choose a room

          Level +2 or +3, north or south side. You get an answer within two weeks.

        4. Lease and deposit

          Sign the student lease, pay the deposit into the blocked account, check the room inventory when you move in.

        5. Move in

          Collect your transponder and get an introduction to the kitchen, bathrooms, study rooms and bicycle store.

        6. Stay or leave

          Renew for the next academic year by Easter. Free rooms go to the waiting list.

        For investors · building, use, income

        Three uses, three income streams

        S7 spreads the risk across a public library, a café and student housing. The library brings visitors and a reliable public tenant; the housing brings most of the rent.

        The building today

        Built1927 to 1930, neo-Romanesque, architect Carlos Malfait, for the Redemptorists
        Vacantsince 2016
        Sizethree aisles, about 40 m deep and 17 m wide, nave 12 m high
        Existing areasground floor 764 m² · level +1 690 m² · attic 282 m² (not accessible today)
        Condition2018: poor roof tiles, damp, loose masonry. 2019: asbestos, probably in the roof boarding

        LocationVoskenslaan, close to the station and the university campuses; the west side faces Clemenspoort
